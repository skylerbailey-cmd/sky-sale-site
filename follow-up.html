02 — Follow-up

Most shops mean to send something. Most shops don't.

Not because they don't want to — because it's a job for later, and later has customers in it. Every sale here can leave the counter with its email already written.

After a sale

Built from what they actually bought.

The difference between an email people read and one they delete is whether it knows anything about them.

The receipt, as the sale closes
Emailed the moment you take payment, carrying the name, address and footer of the shop that made the sale — not whichever account happens to be signed in on that browser.
A welcome email assembled from the receipt
Someone who buys a device and two serums gets guidance on those three things, in that combination — how to use them, in what order, how often. It is the email that brings them back for the refill, and it takes nobody any time to write.
Read it before it goes
Every message opens in a preview with the subject and body editable. Nothing leaves in your name that nobody looked at.
From your mailbox, not ours
Mail sends through your own connected account, so it arrives from your address, replies come back to you, and every message sits in your Sent folder where you'd expect to find it.

Writing to a group

Describe who you mean. It finds them.

Choosing the audience is the whole job, so it happens where you write the email — not in a report somewhere else.

Nine audiences to start from
Everyone · bought in the last 30 days · bought in the last 6 months · haven't been back in 6 months · gone quiet for a year · first bought this month · spent $2,000 or more · birthday this month · bought once and never again. Each is a question a shop actually asks, not a filter to assemble.
Then narrow it
By product, by who served them, by when they last bought, by what they've spent, by birth month. A starting audience plus a product gives you "lapsed customers who bought that device", which is a letter worth writing.
It counts who you can actually reach
Not who matched — who matched and has an email address, with the difference stated. The first dozen are listed by name, so you can see the audience is the one you meant before anything is sent.
You see the first one before you send
A sample shows exactly what the first recipient receives, with their own name filled in — so nobody discovers the personalisation didn't work after writing to four hundred people.
Separate emails, every time
Each person receives their own message addressed to them. Nobody is ever shown anybody else's address.
